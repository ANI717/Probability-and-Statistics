# Bar Graphs

A **bar graph** displays categorical data using rectangular bars.

The height or length of each bar represents the frequency or relative frequency of a category.

## Example

| Pet | Students |
|---|---:|
| Dog | 20 |
| Cat | 15 |
| Fish | 5 |

A bar graph would have three separate bars representing Dog, Cat, and Fish.

## Components

A bar graph usually contains:

- Categories
- Frequency or percentage scale
- Bars
- Axis labels
- Title

## Reading a Bar Graph

Look at the height of each bar to determine the value associated with that category.

Bar graphs make it easy to compare categories.

## Important

Bars are normally separated because the categories are distinct.

This is different from a **histogram**, which represents quantitative numerical intervals and usually has touching bars.

## Comparing Groups

Multiple bars can represent different groups.

Example:

| Pet | Men | Women |
| --- | --: | ----: |
| Dog |  20 |    25 |
| Cat |  10 |    20 |

This allows categories to be compared across groups.
