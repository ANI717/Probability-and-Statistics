# Relationships in Categorical Data

Two categorical variables may have an **association**.

An association exists when the distribution of one variable changes depending on the category of another variable.

## Example

Suppose:

| | Likes Coffee | Doesn't Like Coffee |
|---|---:|---:|
| Men | 60% | 40% |
| Women | 80% | 20% |

Coffee preference differs between men and women.

This suggests an **association** between the variables.

## No Association

Suppose instead:

| | Likes Coffee | Doesn't Like Coffee |
|---|---:|---:|
| Men | 70% | 30% |
| Women | 70% | 30% |

The distributions are identical.

There is no apparent association.

## How to Look for Association

Compare **conditional percentages**, not just raw counts.

Ask:

> Does the distribution of one variable change across groups of the other variable?

Large differences suggest an association.

Similar distributions suggest little or no association.

## Important

**Association does not automatically mean causation.**

Finding that two variables are related does not prove that one caused the other.
