# Unit 01 - Analyzing Categorical Data

## Core Concepts

### Data

**Individual**
→ The person/object/entity being studied.

**Variable**
→ A characteristic recorded about an individual.

### Variable Types

**Categorical**
→ Places observations into categories.

Examples:
- Color
- Country
- Yes/No

**Quantitative**
→ Represents numerical quantities.

Examples:
- Height
- Age
- Weight

---

## Frequency

**Frequency**
→ Number of observations.

**Relative Frequency**
→ Proportion of observations.

Relative Frequency = Frequency / Total

---

## Visualizing One Categorical Variable

### Pictograph

Uses symbols to represent frequencies.

Always check how much each symbol represents.

### Bar Graph

Uses bars to compare frequencies or relative frequencies between categories.

---

## Two Categorical Variables

### Two-Way Frequency Table

Displays frequencies for combinations of two categorical variables.

Contains:

- Joint frequencies
- Marginal frequencies
- Grand total

### Venn Diagram

Visualizes overlapping categories.

### Two-Way Relative Frequency Table

Shows proportions or percentages instead of raw counts.

---

## Distributions

### Marginal Distribution

Distribution of one variable across **all observations**.

Usually:

Marginal Frequency / Grand Total

### Conditional Distribution

Distribution of one variable **within a particular group**.

Usually:

Cell Frequency / Group Total

---

## Association

Two categorical variables are associated when the distribution of one variable differs depending on the category of the other.

Compare **conditional distributions** to look for associations.

Similar conditional distributions
→ little/no evidence of association.

Different conditional distributions
→ evidence of association.

Association ≠ causation.
