# Two-Way Relative Frequency Tables

A **two-way relative frequency table** shows proportions or percentages instead of raw counts.

Start with frequencies:

| | Coffee | No Coffee | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Men | 30 | 20 | 50 |
| Women | 40 | 10 | 50 |
| Total | 70 | 30 | 100 |

Divide each value by the grand total:

| | Coffee | No Coffee | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Men | 30% | 20% | 50% |
| Women | 40% | 10% | 50% |
| Total | 70% | 30% | 100% |

## Formula

Relative Frequency = Cell Frequency / Total Observations

Example:

30 / 100 = 0.30 = 30%

## Why Use Relative Frequency?

Raw counts can be misleading when groups have different sizes.

Relative frequencies allow proportions to be compared.

## Important

A relative frequency may be calculated relative to:

- the entire table
- a row
- a column

The denominator determines what the percentage means.

This becomes especially important for [[Conditional Distributions]].
