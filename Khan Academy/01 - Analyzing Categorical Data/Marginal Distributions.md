# Marginal Distributions

A **marginal distribution** describes the distribution of **one categorical variable by itself**, ignoring the other variable in a two-way table.

Consider:

| | Coffee | No Coffee | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Men | 30 | 20 | 50 |
| Women | 40 | 10 | 50 |
| Total | 70 | 30 | 100 |

## Coffee Preference Distribution

Coffee:

70 / 100 = 70%

No Coffee:

30 / 100 = 30%

Therefore the marginal distribution is:

- Coffee → 70%
- No Coffee → 30%

## Gender Distribution

Men:

50 / 100 = 50%

Women:

50 / 100 = 50%

## Why "Marginal"?

The totals appear in the **margins** of a two-way table.

## Formula

Marginal Relative Frequency = Marginal Frequency / Grand Total

## Key Idea

Marginal distribution:

> Distribution of one variable for everyone.

Conditional distribution:

> Distribution of one variable within a specific group.
