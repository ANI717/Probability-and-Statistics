# Two-Way Frequency Tables

A **two-way frequency table** summarizes the relationship between **two categorical variables**.

Example:

| | Likes Coffee | Doesn't Like Coffee | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Men | 30 | 20 | 50 |
| Women | 40 | 10 | 50 |
| Total | 70 | 30 | 100 |

Variables:

1. Gender
2. Coffee preference

## Joint Frequency

A cell representing the combination of two categories is a **joint frequency**.

Example:

30 men like coffee.

## Marginal Frequency

The totals along the edges of the table are **marginal frequencies**.

Examples:

Total men = 50

Total people who like coffee = 70

## Grand Total

The bottom-right value represents the total number of observations.

Here:

100

## Finding Missing Values

Use row and column totals.

Example:

If:

Total Men = 50

Men who like coffee = 30

then:

Men who don't like coffee = 50 - 30 = 20
